Website Design Options - Pick Your Direction

💡 Design Strategy Notes

Context: Jacob & Alicia's restaurant is located inside a convenience store in St. Paul. They've been open about a year, serving pickup and delivery only. The food is authentic, the story is real (love, 15 kids, living the dream), and early Google reviews are strong.

The Goal: The homepage needs to be their "storefront" - since they have no dine-in, the website must hook visitors immediately and make them want to order.

Traditional & Vibrant

Version 1: Cultural Fire

"Authentic Mexican Energy"

This design celebrates traditional Mexican aesthetics with vibrant colors, papel picado patterns, Talavera tiles, and warm cultural elements. The hero features a dynamic split layout with massive food photography meeting hand-painted mural vibes.

🎯 Best For: Families, traditional customers, locals who appreciate authentic Mexican culture

✓ Strengths:

  • Immediately communicates authenticity
  • Warm, welcoming, family-friendly vibe
  • Honors Mexican heritage and tradition
  • Broad demographic appeal
  • Makes the "hidden gem" story compelling

Upscale & Modern

Version 2: Dark Cinematic

"Netflix Chef's Table Vibes"

A sophisticated, dark, moody design that lets the food photography be the star. Think Netflix food documentaries meets high-end steakhouse. Elegant serif headlines, gold accents, and dramatic lighting elevate the brand.

🎯 Best For: Foodies, upscale customers, date nights, competing with established restaurants

✓ Strengths:

  • Elevates brand perception significantly
  • Food photography takes center stage
  • Positions as "serious culinary experience"
  • Makes convenience store location irrelevant
  • Appeals to premium customers

Bold & Disruptive

Version 3: Street Art Wild

"Supreme Meets Street Tacos"

An unapologetically bold design mixing graffiti art, neon accents, comic book energy, and pop culture vibes. This isn't your typical restaurant website - it's Instagram-worthy, youth-focused, and designed to go viral.

🎯 Best For: Gen Z, Millennials, Instagram crowd, anyone tired of boring restaurant sites

✓ Strengths:

  • Stands out MASSIVELY from competition
  • High viral/shareability potential
  • Attracts younger demographic
  • Memorable and unique brand identity
  • Perfect for social media marketing

🎯 My Honest Recommendation

Version 1 (Cultural Fire) is the safest choice with broad appeal. It honors the authentic story and appeals to families.

Version 2 (Dark Cinematic) is the smart business move if you want to compete with established restaurants and elevate perception. It makes the convenience store location irrelevant because the food looks so damn good.

Version 3 (Street Art Wild) is the risky move that could pay off HUGE. It's polarizing but memorable. If you want to attract a younger crowd and go viral on social media, this is it. But it won't appeal to everyone.

💡 Bottom Line: I'd go with Version 2 (Dark Cinematic) because it elevates the brand, makes the food the hero, and positions Taquería Doña Alicia as a serious culinary destination - not just "a taco spot in a convenience store."
